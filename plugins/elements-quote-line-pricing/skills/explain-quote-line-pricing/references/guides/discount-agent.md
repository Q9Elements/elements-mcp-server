# Discount Agent guide

Salesforce CPQ discount evidence comes from `discounts`, `pricingMethod.type`, `priceWaterfall.stages`, `line.quantity`, `line.effectiveQuantity`, `product`, `quote`, and `contractedPrice`. Prices in the waterfall are per unit; percentages are 0 to 100. Explain only supported changes from the recorded stages, and distinguish a blocked discount from a zero value and an unreadable value.

- `discounts.option` identifies the option percentage or per-unit amount and its source. An option discount affects Special, not Regular. For a blocked option discount, explain `statusReason`, which identifies the quote line or product non-discountable flag. The Bundle and Options Agent explains its bundle context; this agent owns the arithmetic.
- `discounts.quantitySchedule` and `discounts.termSchedule` provide the basis, source, schedule type, tiers, matched tier, units and status. `applied` means a schedule applies; `no_tier_matched` means no tier matched; `price_tier` is a price, not a percentage discount; `none` is absence and `not_readable` is unknown. Range uses the matched tier; Slab can apply across tiers. Check `unitsCheck` and `recordedTierMatchesDerived`; do not present a tier breakdown as reliable when tier units fail to cover the basis. Use `scheduleReconciliation.matchesRegular` and its candidate causes when the derived result does not match Regular: aggregation or a different effective term may explain it, but is not proven. Source and user-defined schedule fields identify the recorded schedule, not an invented origin.
- Under Block pricing, a slab schedule can represent overage; describe that briefly and defer the base calculation to the Pricing Method Agent. A compound discount takes precedence over a schedule when both are set. The compound discount is not collected; name it as a possible cause only when a schedule result does not match Regular.
- `discounts.additional` distinguishes percent and amount, source, unit and surcharge. A negative discount is a surcharge. For a percentage, Customer is Regular multiplied by one minus the percentage divided by 100; an amount reduces the price by that amount, subject to its unit and possible proration. Compare the supplied expected values and recorded Customer rather than assuming which amount convention applies.
- The analysis assumes CPQ's standard discount order (additional, then partner, then distributor); if your org changes that order in CPQ package settings, the step amounts can differ. Check `discounts.partner` and `discounts.distributor` for source, percentage, discountability and expected price. Under the standard order, Net is Partner multiplied by one minus the distributor percentage divided by 100.

`contractedPrice` identifies a contracted discount when present; it affects Special, not the later manual discount. For each block, `applied`, `zero`, `blocked`, and `not_readable` have distinct meanings. Do not claim that a discount applied merely because a field or schedule exists. When none applied and the relevant evidence is available, say so.
